Team advances to finals

For generations, the Boston Red Sox had become accustomed to heartbreak. After winning the World Series in 1918, the famed franchise endured one of the most unprecedented droughts in American sports history. Game 7 losses in 1946, 1967, 1975 and 1986 made it feel as though the Sox might never get over the hump again and lift an elusive sixth Commissioner's Trophy.

By 2004, that barren spell had stretched to a staggering 86 years, reinforcing the idea that Boston was somehow cursed - a notion that had become part of baseball folklore. And when the Sox fell behind 3-0 to the New York Yankees in the American League Championship Series (ALCS), it looked as though history was about to repeat itself.

This gave birth to the “Curse of the Bambino,” the notion that Boston’s decision to part ways with Ruth “is the reason why the Red Sox go not only so long without winning a World Series, but losing them in such catastrophic, fateful, cursive ways,” says Devin Gordon, a contributing writer at The New York Times Magazine.

Hopelessness was probably at a high.
-Joe Posnanski

After Bill Buckner’s infamous error in Game 6 of the 1986 World Series – with the Sox leading the New York Mets 3-2 in the series and 5-3 in the 10th inning with two outs it felt as though this cursed team from New England was just destined to always fall short. As CNN Contributor Bob Costas says in the series, “The story of the Red Sox is heartache… so close, yet so far,” and even 2003, Boston’s gut-wrenching loss to the Yankees in Game 7 of the ALCS showed the “Curse of the Bambino” had followed the Sox into the new millennium.

So when the two franchises met in the ALCS a year later, it seemed almost inevitable that the Red Sox would come up short again, because as Joe Posnanski – author of Why We Love Baseball – highlights, “hopelessness was probably at a high.”
