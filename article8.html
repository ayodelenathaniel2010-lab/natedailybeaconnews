Museum unveils new wing for local arists

During a visit to NASA’s Jet Propulsion Laboratory three years ago, I stepped into a blinding white room. There, I came face-to-face with a space instrument that could one day be used to search for evidence of life beyond Earth. Each person who enters the clean room at the Pasadena, California, facility must wear a white “bunny suit” that covers virtually everything but their eyes.

The instrument, called a coronagraph, is so sensitive to contamination that I was given a list of things forbidden from entering the hallowed space — no makeup, lotion or scented products of any kind could be on my skin.

The instrument team invited me to step inside the coronagraph virtually by equipping me with a pair of VR glasses to see all its components and how they ultimately fit together. This crucial sci-fi-like process is employed early on when assembling such a complex feat of engineering.

Meeting people who had spent years developing the device — and hearing their hopes for what it could reveal to us — opened my eyes to the extreme dedication it takes to build a single space mission. Now, the coronagraph is ready to study the cosmos aboard the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope, which could launch as soon as this weekend. “Roman will investigate dark matter, dark energy and the structure of the universe itself, and accelerate the future of discovery of potentially habitable planets outside our solar system,” said NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman when the completed telescope was unveiled in April.
